**Narrative:**

Fair challenge from the author on `estimate_lris_exposure()` — it was a

adapted from an equally hand-wavy Kast heuristic via an arbitrary "LRIS is

per-amp gain/read-noise/dark-current for LRIS and Kast, extinction/sky-model
data — but nothing wires them into a predictive exposure-time tool).

In parallel, web-searched and found the real answer: UCO/Lick Observatories
hosts a live, physically-grounded LRIS exposure-time calculator at
etc.ucolick.org — same backend serving the official Kast/DEIMOS/ESI/HIRES
ETCs too. Fetched the HTML form and discovered its POST target
(`web_s2n/gen_inst_s2n`) returns JSON directly, no auth, no scraping needed —
a genuine scriptable API, not just a browser tool. Verified by direct POST
testing (not just reading the form) that model coverage has real gaps: the
blue-grism dropdown only *works* for B300/B600 — B400 (our previously
documented 400/3400 config) and B1200 silently return an empty spectrum with
`errormsg=""`, no error at all. Also hit a TLS chain issue (server omits its
intermediate cert) — checked with `openssl s_client -showcerts` that the leaf
cert is genuinely CA-issued (InCommon/Sectigo) rather than assuming it was
safe to just disable verification, then fetched the one missing intermediate
from the cert's AIA CA-issuers URL and built a proper combined trust bundle
(`scripts/certs/ca_bundle_with_incommon.pem`).

Surfaced both real findings to the author before writing the replacement:
which blue grism to standardize on (given B400/B1200 don't work), and what
target S/N should define "enough" exposure (the old heuristic never had one).
The author confirmed the actual standing LRIS setup is grism 600/4000, grating
600/7500 centered ~7200Å (both ETC-supported — good), and target S/N = 5 per
pixel continuum.

Built `scripts/lris_etc.py`: `query_lris_etc()` POSTs to the real ETC,
`required_exptime()` iterates real ETC calls (S/N≈√t only picks the next
value to try, never assumed as final) to hit the target S/N in blue
(4200-5200Å) and red (6500-7500Å) windows separately, and
`estimate_lris_exposure_real()` takes the longer-required arm as the
simultaneous wall-clock time, split into ≥2 sub-exposures ≤600s each. Wired
it into `keck_2026oct02_calculations.py` in place of the bucket table
(passing each target's real airmass), corrected the LRIS Configuration
tables in both scripts and the summary doc (600/4000 blue, 600/7500 red —
was wrongly 400/3400/400/8500), and reran the full pipeline.

Result was a large correction, not a tweak: total science time for the same
4 targets dropped from 3.3h to 0.6h (34 min) — real LRIS exposures at S/N=5
are 7-11 min each including overhead, not 45-55 min. This tracks: Keck's 10m
vs. Lick's 3m is ~11x the collecting area, and the old heuristic's flat "2x"
fudge factor was never going to be close. Also checked (not just assumed)
that the ETC's lack of a moon-phase term doesn't matter for this specific
night — the Moon is ~48% illuminated but below the horizon (alt ≈ -17°) at
local midnight on Oct 2/3, 2026.

New implication documented for the author: with real (short) exposure times,
the plan's 6% utilization is now limited by target *count*, not exposure
time — recommended requesting a much larger `num_targ_longslit` in future
