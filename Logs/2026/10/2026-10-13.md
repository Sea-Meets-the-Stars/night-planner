## 03:49 UT

**Prompt:** Trying to add rejected targets with less available time is not working,

**Model:** claude-opus-5. **Tokens/CO2:** see `Logs/log_summary.csv` (authoritative).

Reproduced it against tonight's Lick-2026B-03 plan. `FRB20260619A` is rejected
with "needs 40 min but only 21 min left in its HA window" -- exactly the case
forcing exists for. Posting it to `/api/regenerate` with 21 min returned the
same eight-target schedule and no complaint: the force was silently dropped.

Two faults, one of them hiding the other.

The real one: both `kast_calculations.append_forced()` and Keck's
`append_forced_targets()` place a forced target *after* the whole schedule.
That only works for a target still up at the end of the night -- which is how
the feature was first used (FRB20250411A rising into the last 80 min on
2026-10-06). The targets actually worth rescuing are the opposite case: a
fast-setting host dropped because its full exposure wouldn't fit the sliver of
window left. By the time the schedule ends, that window shut hours ago, so the
append could never succeed. FRB20260619A's window closes at 20:40; the cursor
reached it at 04:29.

The second: `adapters.lick_kast()` never read `out['forced_failures']`, so even
the "window closed" message the scheduler printed never reached the browser.
Keck's adapter already did this; Lick's didn't.

Fix for Lick: `reserve_forced()` replaces `append_forced()`. It places each
forced target inside its own window *before* the greedy pass, and hands the
slot back as a blocked window (padded by one slew either side) so
`schedule_greedy` fills the night around it. Placement is as late inside the
window as the requested time allows -- one rule that covers both halves: a
setting target lands at the top of the night because that is all it has, a
rising one lands at the end where it was going anyway, and either way the
reservation eats the least of everyone else's time. Result: 9 targets,
FRB20260619A at 20:20-20:40 with 21 of 25 min, nothing else displaced.

Keck needed more care. The same reservation approach, applied bluntly, cost
FRB20240106A on the already-delivered 2026-10-06 plan: reserving a worst-case
20-min slew ahead of FRB20250411A pushed its block back to 03:09, five minutes
inside FRB20240106A's 03:24 finish. Shrinking the pad to the base 10-min slew
didn't quite recover it either, because placing the forced target at
latest-in-window (03:29) is five minutes earlier than the append had put it
(03:34 -- which overran the AM<2.0 window by five minutes, something the old
code explicitly permitted).

So Keck now tries both, in order: append first, because it disturbs nothing;
fall back to a reserved slot and re-run the whole night only for the targets
append can't place. `select_targets_for_night()` gained `reserved`/
`reservations` parameters and calls itself once for the second pass, clearing
`skip_log` on the way because pass 1's skips describe a night that no longer
exists. Regenerating Keck-2026B-02 now reproduces the delivered schedule
exactly, timestamp for timestamp.

Verified the failure path too: forcing FRB20260514A (never HA-legal at Lick
tonight) now surfaces as a `4. Forced` rejection reading "forced but not
placed -- not observable tonight -- never inside the Shane HA window", instead
of vanishing. 24 tests pass. Tested the force through the live API and then
regenerated tonight's plan without it, so what's on disk still matches the
finders and starlist already generated -- the decision to actually add
FRB20260619A is the author's, and it needs a finder and a starlist rebuild
after.

## 08:05 UT

**Prompt:** why are the secondaries not labeled in our finder charts, can you remake them with the secondaries labeled

**Model:** claude-opus-5. **Tokens/CO2:** see `Logs/log_summary.csv` (authoritative).

They weren't labelled because they were never drawn. `night_planner/finder.py`
only ever put three things on a chart -- the target, a Gaia offset star, and
the slit -- which was right when it was written for an arbitrary target table,
where the given coordinates simply *are* the target. But Lick-2026B-03's table
carries the full PATH output, `Sec_name/Sec_RA/Sec_Dec/Sec_mag/Sec_POx`, for
all 19 targets, and the runner-up candidate is exactly what you want to see at
the eyepiece: it says how crowded the field is and which object you are *not*
pointing at.

Added `load_secondaries()`, which reads those columns out of the plan's own
target table (so an offline table carries its own candidates and nothing is
asked of FFFF-PZ), and `_plan_targets_csv()` in the backend to recover which
table a saved plan came from via its recorded `request.argv`. Colour follows
`chime_ffff_pz/finder.py`: red primary, orange secondary, cyan offset star.

Two rounds of fixing after looking at the output, which is the part worth
recording.

First render used `ax.scatter` with markers sized in *points*. FRB20260702C's
secondary is 2.8" away; at a 4' field that is seven pixels, so the two 260-pt
circles merged into one blob with "P" and "S" printed on top of each other --
a chart that actively misleads. Switched to `SphericalCircle` at a fixed 2"
sky radius (what chime_ffff_pz does, and for this reason), and pushed the two
letters apart along the line joining the pair.

That was honest but still unreadable: a 2.8" pair genuinely cannot be resolved
at full field. So close pairs (< 12") now also get a zoom inset -- a `Cutout2D`
around the midpoint, box = 6x the separation, purple frame, both rings redrawn
at lw=2. In the inset the labels go vertically rather than radially; the
radial offsets that work at full field land on the inset's frame, because the
inset is small in figure units while the offsets are in points.

Also switched the caption's P(O|x) to `:.3g`: FRB20260708D's secondary has
P(O|x) = 3.8e-279, which `:.3f` rendered as "0.000".

All eight of tonight's charts regenerated with the secondary ringed, named,
magnitude in the legend, and separation + P(O|x) in the caption. Three got an
inset (2.8", 2.9", 4.4" -- and 9.4" for FRB20260708D). The finder JSON
sidecars gained `secondary_*` fields; the starlist reads only `slit_pa` and
the offset-star keys, so it does not need regenerating. 24 tests pass.

## 09:20 UT

**Prompt:** ok, sometimes we want the top two candidates on the slit regardless of the dispersion, so when generating finder charts, have an option to ask user whether we want top two in the slit or paralactic, also, does this paralactic take into consideration exposure time? because that changes for longer exposures

**Model:** claude-opus-5. **Tokens/CO2:** see `Logs/log_summary.csv` (authoritative).

Two things, one build and one answer.

**The option.** There was already a "use FFFF-PZ" checkbox that fits the slit
through two candidates, but it shells out to `chime_ffff_pz_finder`, needs the
other environment and a live Resource, and uses FFFF-PZ's candidate list
rather than the plan's. Since yesterday's work put `Sec_RA`/`Sec_Dec` into the
finder's hands, the same thing can be done locally: `generate_for_plan()` and
the `/api/finders/generate` endpoint now take `slit_mode`, either
`'parallactic'` (unchanged default) or `'candidates'`, which sets the slit to
`primary.position_angle(secondary)`. The FFFF-PZ checkbox stays, relabelled to
say what actually distinguishes it.

The thing worth being careful about is that this is a real trade, not a free
option, so neither the chart nor the sidecar is allowed to hide it. Every
chart in candidates mode prints how far off parallactic it ended up, in the
subtitle and again in the caption ("both candidates; parallactic would be
108.9"), and the sidecar records `slit_mode`, `parallactic_pa` and
`pa_vs_parallactic`. A target with no secondary falls back to parallactic and
the browser says which ones did -- silently returning a parallactic slit when
the observer asked for both candidates would be the worst outcome of all,
since they'd dial in an angle they didn't choose.

Tonight's three test cases show the spread: FRB20260702C costs **0.4 deg**
(the pair happens to lie along the parallactic direction), FRB20230805A costs
**43.6 deg**, FRB20260623C costs **59.5 deg**. So it is per-target, and the
chart is what tells you whether to take the deal.

**The answer.** Yes -- `average_parallactic()` is already integrated over
`target.science_start` to `target.end`, i.e. that target's own scheduled
block, sampled every 5 min and weighted by tan(z). It is not a single-instant
angle. Added `pa_vs_exposure()` to `scripts/analyze_slit_pa.py` to show the
dependence rather than assert it; for FRB20230805A starting 00:10 the chosen
angle runs 59.8 deg at 20 min, 51.9 at 60, 45.2 at 90, 38.1 at 120, and the
weighted misalignment goes 2.3 -> 7.2 -> 11.9 -> 17.6 deg. The real lesson is
in the derivative, not the number: past about 90 min on a target like this one
a single slit setting stops being defensible and the block wants splitting.
The corollary is that finders are tied to the scheduled exposure -- change a
duration and they need regenerating.

24 tests pass. Tonight's charts untouched; the candidates-mode test ran into
the scratch directory.
