"Lick" here means Kast, whose target S/N per sub-exposure was set to 5 "for
now" earlier on 2026-11-07. The single source of truth is `TARGET_SN` in
`night_planner/etc.py`; `instrument_params.resolve_options` falls back to it
when `--target-sn` is not given. I changed it to 10 and brought the strings
that quote it in line: the `--target-sn` help text, the README flags table,
the `make_kast_exposure` docstring in `scripts/kast_calculations.py`, and
the assertion in `tests/test_etc_airmass.py` that checks the plan's
assumptions say "5 …" (now "10 …").

An important caveat: Kast still defaults to the empirical magnitude rule,
which has no S/N term, so default Kast plans do not change. S/N 10 only
applies with `--etc kast` (or a custom ETC).

The first test run showed 22 failures and 11 errors. They were all astropy
IERS "interpolating from IERS_Auto using predictive values" errors, because
the sandbox cannot download a fresh IERS table, so they had nothing to do
with the change. Rerunning through `pytest.main` with
`iers.conf.auto_max_age = None` and auto-download off gave 144 passed
(network tests deselected).

To show what S/N 10 means in practice, I reran
`scripts/compare_kast_exposure_models.py --target-sn 10` against the real
UCO ETC (9 magnitudes, airmass 1.2, seeing 1.5″). I wrote the output to a new
file, `Night_plans/kast_exposure_model_comparison_sn10.csv`, and kept the S/N 5
table. Results: 3 min at r = 16 (rule: 40), 43 min at r = 19 (rule: 60),
158 min at r = 20 (rule: 90). From r = 20.5 it hits the 3 × 7200 s cap, and
at r = 21 it reaches only S/N ≈ 7. Roughly 4× the S/N 5 times, as S/N ∝ √t
predicts for background-limited faint targets. This makes the open
"Kast default: empirical vs ETC" decision sharper: at S/N 10 the ETC would
make r ≳ 20 hosts impractical on Kast.
