**Inventory.** Lordrick had already copied `Night_plans/` to `pvt_Nighplans/`
(`diff -rq` shows they are identical), and `paths.py` already had a
`NIGHT_PLANNER_PLANS` hook. To find leaks beyond the plan directories, I wrote

(RA, Dec) string pairs from the private dir, then checks every HEAD file and
every blob in all 36 commits on all branches. It reads office-zip XML and PDF
text as well as plain text.

The first runs had false positives. Synthetic SIM plans contributed made-up
J-names; flux standards spelled in lower case in starlists (`bd284211`)
slipped past a case-sensitive filter; and `cand_gal_names` cells are
Python-list strings, which fused several names together. After the fixes:
1650 names, 817 positions, 235 HEAD files and 239 history paths.

Beyond `Night_plans/` and `Keck-2026B-02_test1000/`, the leaks are in the 3
run prompts, 13 run-specific scripts, 15 daily logs, the brain, README, docs,

two tests (`test_targets_io.py`, `test_backend.py`) that hold real positions

`tests/test_data` hits are a coincidence: the synthetic generator's
`FRB2026MMDDx` names match two real TNS names, with fake positions. The PPTX
and PDF files and the commit messages are clean.

directories, plus `--replace-text`, which turns names into stable pseudonyms

`pseudonyms.csv` key. I applied the rules in memory to every surviving file and
rescanned: zero names, zero positions left. The full step list is in the

I recommended yes. Making the repo private is cheap and can be undone, and it
stops new exposure while the decisions in D1–D4 are pending. It does not

stars and watchers may be lost; and Actions minutes become limited. Making it
private does not remove the need for the history rewrite before the repo goes
public again.
